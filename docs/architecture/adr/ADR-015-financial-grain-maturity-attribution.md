# ADR-015 — Financial grain, maturity and attribution

Status: Accepted for implementation (owner decision record 2026-09-28: D-12, D-14, D-15, D-16 delegated to engineering; D-13 owner-accepted recommendation). Date: 2026-09-28.

## Context

ADR-002 and ADR-003 fixed the principles (one additive charge truth, attribution separate, three status axes) but not the numbers and keys where money is computed. The review found that estimates cannot be replaced key-for-key by billing buckets, FINAL has no horizons, long queries break both extraction and hourly attribution, no default allocation exists for cloud services, storage and fees, the rule engine has no execution model, and allocation has no input grain ([audit](../../22-implementation-readiness/AUDIT_CROSS_CUTTING.md) X-18…X-22; blockers G-FIN-01 and G-ALC-01). This ADR consolidates the accepted decisions; the amendments of [ADR-002](ADR-002-financial-grains.md) and [ADR-003](ADR-003-maturity-and-close.md) point here. Normative detail: [FIN backlog](../../22-implementation-readiness/backlog/FIN.md) §3.1–§3.6 and [ALC backlog](../../22-implementation-readiness/backlog/ALC.md) G-ALC-01…05, G-ALC-10.

## Decision

1. **Canonical charge grain and supersession (D-12).** `fct_charge` identity is the fine billing bucket (tenant, organization, contract, scope_kind, account nullable only for organization scope, usage_date UTC, service_type, usage_type, rating_type, billing_type, balance_source, is_adjustment, currency, basis AUTH/EST). Estimates are produced only at the family bucket (tenant, organization, scope_kind, account, usage_date, service_family, currency). Supersession per family bucket follows the eight-case decision table of FIN §3.1: an accepted authoritative row deactivates the estimate for its family bucket; a mature (+72 h) daily billing snapshot deactivates all estimates for that account-day; metered usage without billing after maturity keeps a PROVISIONAL estimate flagged `BILLING_MISSING` and fails control C2; tenants without billing access use customer-approved rates (`CUSTOMER_APPROVED_RATE`, FIN-105) and never claim `BILLED_SOURCE`. Money comes only from the authoritative normalizer and the estimator; per-service models are attribution models. Spend is the sum of all funding classes (capacity, rollover, free, rebate, on-demand) with a funding breakdown.
2. **Maturity horizons (D-13).** Versioned per-source policy (FIN-104): metering +24 h, daily metering +24 h, rate sheet +48 h, currency billing +72 h; query attribution FINAL at hour end + T + 24 h, T = min(maximum statement timeout, 48 h), i.e. 72 h by default; Adaptive query metering at hour end + 24 h unless a running query overlaps the hour. FINAL also requires contiguous coverage. `MONTH_STABLE` (period_stability = STABLE) at month end + 5 days (configurable, re-derived by FIN-108) is distinct from daily FINAL and gates close.
3. **Temporal attribution of long queries (D-14).** Each query's attributed credits are prorated over metering hours by execution overlap: credits × |[start, end) ∩ [h, h+1)| / (end − start); a zero-duration query goes to its start hour. Idle per hour = warehouse compute − warehouse attributed credits. `PRORATION_RESIDUAL_h` = warehouse attributed credits − Σ prorated query credits is an explicit, signed attribution row (short queries absent from QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY land there). Example: 2.0 credits from 23:30 to 01:30 → 0.5 / 1.0 / 0.5, so day 1 = 0.5 and day 2 = 1.5. Extraction must capture long queries by completion time ([ADR-006 amendment](ADR-006-batch-commit-and-coverage.md)).
4. **Exact money plumbing.** Internal scale NUMBER(38,12); Python `Decimal` context with precision 76 and trapped inexact conversions; `allocate_exact` (floor to 1e-12, distribute remaining quanta by largest remainder, stable key; children sum exactly to parent; explicit `UNATTRIBUTED` residual when detail quantity is below the bucket quantity); statement rounding `round_statement_lines` = sign-normalized signed largest remainder in minor units with the total rounded half-up; JSON money is a plain decimal string, never "-0". The daily effective rate is display-only.
5. **Default allocation book (D-15).** Seeded at onboarding and editable (ALC-103): warehouse compute by query cost; idle proportional to consumers, falling back to warehouse owner then UNALLOCATED; cloud services proportional to gross cloud-services credits (requires `CREDITS_USED_CLOUD_SERVICES` in the query projection frozen by ING-101; until then a labelled query-cost share); storage by database owner (DATABASE_STORAGE_USAGE_HISTORY + AU.DATABASES); serverless by owning object; transfer, replication, organization fees and rebates to a platform bucket; everything else, including unmapped services, UNALLOCATED. The billed cloud-services adjustment exists only at (account, UTC day); no per-query 10 % deduction.
6. **Rule evaluation (D-16).** Rules compile to rows of versioned predicate tables (dimension, operator, value, priority, rule_version) in disjunctive normal form and are evaluated for all tenants by one generic set-based SQL model; no per-tenant code generation and no Python evaluation. R1 operators: `eq`, `in`, `prefix`, `suffix`, `contains`, `is_null` and their negations; `regex` is R2 after cost testing. A NULL attribute is false for every operator except `is_null`. Subject levels resolve as OVERRIDE > ACTIVITY > OBJECT > CONTAINER > inherited carrier > UNCLASSIFIED, priority compared within a level; conflicts discovered after publication never pick a winner (subject → UNASSIGNED with status CONFLICT). User-name predicates accept only `eq`/`in`, compiled to pseudonyms (D-10).
7. **Allocation input and restricted output.** `int_alloc_unit` splits each charge into QUERY, IDLE, HOUR_RESIDUAL, RESOURCE or WHOLE units that sum exactly to the charge and is the only allocation input. Allocation output keeps `component_kind`. Team-restricted readers see only a restricted projection (allocated amount, method, status) without source amounts, weights or denominators, so sibling spend cannot be derived (G-ALC-03).
8. **Reconciliation and close.** Controls C1–C9 (FIN §3.4) with `delta = ledger − reference`; PENDING / MATCHED / WARNING (approved classified explanation only) / FAILED; no balancing plug. Invoice and usage-statement intake with maker-checker (FIN-101); for capacity contracts the comparable reference is the monthly usage statement. Close, restatement and carry-forward follow FIN §3.5 with maker-checker and a pinned freeze set. All financial periods are UTC.
9. **Coverage scope (D-20, D-35).** Financial coverage of every billed service family is R1 through the bucket ledger; attribution detail for Adaptive, Streaming, QAS, Cortex, SPCS, marketplace and replication is R1 per D-20. Snowflake trial accounts are excluded from reconciliation claims, close and chargeback.

## Alternatives considered

Key-based 1:1 replacement of estimates (double counts 410 instead of 210 on overage days). One pricing join per service model (ten places for rate ambiguity). Allocating on `charge_id` alone (idle and query-cost policies become meaningless). Start-date attribution of long queries (negative idle). A per-query 10 % cloud-services deduction (phantom cost for small-query teams). Python rule evaluation (violates PRD §2.5) or per-tenant generated SQL (per-tenant compilation). Largest remainder on absolute amounts then reapplying sign (mixed-sign statements do not add up).

## Consequences

`fct_charge` rows are few and stable; resource-level money depends on attribution coverage, which is reported rather than hidden. Monitors default to PROVISIONAL data (D-27). The spend-band metering of the plan model (D-17, LCH-105) reads MONTH_STABLE totals. Tenant-zero verification (FIN-108) must confirm enum strings, storage accrual and month revision lag before crosswalk v1 and maturity policy v1 are frozen.

## Revisit conditions

Measured lateness or revision lag differs from the defaults; Snowflake changes billing dimensions or the cloud-services rule (detected by informational control `CS_ADJUSTMENT_RULE`); regex operators pass cost testing; a customer requires cash-effective (funding-aware) views.
